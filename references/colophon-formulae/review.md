# Colophon formulae: review list

96 entries grouped by language. The inventory includes formula families, constructions, markers, and explicitly discussed examples; it is not a count of independently established recurrent formulae.

Attributions describe the chapter’s claims. They do not certify that a scholar first identified an expression. Source line numbers refer to the supplied pasted chapter. No website files are changed by this inventory.

## Attribution issues requiring review

- The chapter mentions Reynhout’s twenty-nine representative formulae, but does not enumerate all twenty-nine. Only expressions actually supplied in the chapter are included.
- The prose and final table disagree about **Que/Qui me scribebat … nomen habebat** and **Finitus est iste liber per me soror**.
- **Iste liber scripsit** is marked Reynhout in the final table without formula-specific support in the prose.
- **Cuius animae propitietur Deus** and **Nomen scriptoris … plenus amoris** receive contradictory corpus-status and attribution statements.
- **Finis adest operis; mercedem posco laboris** follows the coherent wording already supplied by the chapter; “merce dem poseo” is flagged as a draft corruption.
- **Pour ses soeurs** is a dedication to the sisters in the cited example, not an explicit prayer request.
- **Bidt vor die schrivers** retains the chapter’s German label, with its Low German / Middle Dutch placement flagged.
- The Moreton bibliography uses both an edited-volume reference dated 2023 and the key `kiraz_nun-scribes_2024`; Arthur lacks a formula-specific reference.
- The final comparison table leaves the Moreton column blank although the prose explicitly associates several formula families with her work.
- Several extended examples occur only in working notes. These are marked individually. Prayer names, short markers, and non-verbal signs require different treatment from full formulae in website matching.

## Website treatment after review

- Display the scholarly association and its uncertainty with each expression.
- Keep formula families, short markers, and individually attested expressions distinguishable.
- Show the text that supports a match. Broad words such as `onore`, `syster`, `freira`, or `iste liber` alone do not establish the longer formulae containing them.
- Match recorded variations without silently correcting transmitted readings. Keep complete formula matches distinguishable from abbreviated or related expressions.
- Read repeated transcription fields, rather than only the first field. Establish the counting unit and corpus coverage before reporting occurrence totals.
- Treat contextual Latin tags within vernacular colophons as hybrid evidence; the language of a matched phrase is not automatically the language of the whole colophon.
- Do not import the working chapter’s counts or absence claims as calculated website results.
